multiview: two cameras, interactively

Two stationary cameras watch an overlapping volume; geometry turns their flat, depthless pixel grids into a metric 3D model. These two interactive pages teach how — every number on them is computed live, in plain JavaScript, from the same equations the accompanying document derives and the C99 library implements.

1  Two cameras watching a molecule →

Start here: a solved rig is handed to you. Drag the cameras and the molecule and watch projection, epipolar lines, plane-homography transfer with its parallax residue, triangulation, and the depth-error law respond — the displays are deliberately coarse so pixel quantization is visible everywhere.

2  Earn the rig →

Then take the rig away and solve it yourself: bank varied poses of a box of known size, run the eight-point/essential-matrix pipeline on the marks both cameras see, anchor the metre with one known edge — and measure how the solved rig differs from truth before using it to triangulate a point cloud and a TSDF.

Deeper reading: the full document multiview.pdf (theory as a self-study overview with hand-computable worked examples, measured results, and bibliography) and the source repository, where each public header names the document section it implements.

Static pages, no build step and no dependencies; the math lives in web/model.js, unit-tested headlessly in the repo's make check.
