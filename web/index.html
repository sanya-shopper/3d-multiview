Two cameras watching a molecule

A camera reduces a 3D scene to a grid of pixels — depthless and unitless on its own; a second camera restores depth, and calibration gives the pixels metric meaning. Interactive companion to doc/multiview.pdf: everything below is computed live from the document's equations — projection (§4), plane-homography transfer (§5.1), epipolar geometry (§6), triangulation (§7), depth error (§8).

What the panels show, in detail

A stylized molecule graph floats in the working volume, observed by the two cameras of a stationary rig. Click an atom in a camera view to select it (yellow); the dashed cyan line in each view is the selected pixel's epipolar line from the other view — the 1D locus where its match must lie. The predicted panel warps camera 1's display into camera 2's frame by the computed plane homography; yellow rings mark where camera 2 actually sees each atom, and the cyan cross is the depth-true transfer of the selected pixel. The diff panel shows |actual − predicted| per pixel (×2 gain): dark where the plane transfer works, glowing where parallax — the depth signal no single 2D matrix can model — remains. The glow is large by design: the default rig is strongly converged (B ≈ 3.6 m), so off-plane atoms miss by ~9 px on a 64-px display, and thin strokes saturate the difference; doc §6.1 Plane transfer and parallax derives the numbers. Shrink the baseline and watch the glow fade; the displays are deliberately coarse (64×48) so pixel quantization, the σd of §8's error law, is visible everywhere.

With tracking on, drag a camera dot anywhere — it stays aimed. Dragging inside a camera view aims it by hand (turns tracking off).


Scenes are URL-addressable (doc §1.1): ?scene=default, ?scene=narrow, ?scene=roll, or paste a copied link to reproduce any arrangement.
