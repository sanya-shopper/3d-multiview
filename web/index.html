Two cameras watching a molecule

An interactive companion to doc/multiview.pdf: a stylized molecule graph floats in the working volume, observed by the two cameras of a stationary rig. Everything the page computes is a calculation the document derives — pinhole projection (§4), plane-homography view transfer (§5.1), the fundamental matrix and epipolar lines (§6), DLT triangulation (§7), and the depth-error law (§8) — recomputed live as you move the cameras or the molecule.

Drag a camera view to turn that camera; aim at molecule re-points it. Drag the molecule in the world view (drag = move, shift-drag = rotate, alt/option-drag = depth), or use the sliders. Click an atom in a camera view to select it (yellow); the dashed cyan line in each view is the selected pixel's epipolar line from the other view. The fourth panel warps camera 1's display into camera 2's frame by the computed plane homography — compare it against the actual camera 2 panel: the leftover misalignment (yellow rings mark truth) is parallax, which no single 2D matrix can transfer and which depth recovery lives on.

        Make the baseline small (move the cameras together) and watch the
        triangulation error grow as the rays intersect at grazing angles
        (§10); watch the predicted panel converge to the actual one as the
        baseline shrinks, since parallax vanishes with it.
