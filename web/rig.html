Earn the rig: solving two cameras from a known box

The molecule page hands you a solved rig; this page makes you earn it. Two static cameras watch a parallelepiped of known dimensions (1.20 × 0.80 × 0.50 m), carrying 20 printed marks (8 corners, 12 edge midpoints); each of its six faces wears its own color, so you can tell at a glance which side the cameras are seeing. Phase 1 solves the rig from the box's mark pixels alone — normalized 8-point (§6), essential-matrix decomposition, and the known 1200 mm edge to anchor the metre (§1) — and shows how the solved baseline, rotation, and the box's other dimensions differ from ground truth. Phase 2 measures with the solved rig: move the box and triangulated corners (§7) accumulate a point cloud and a scatter-updated TSDF (§3.5, §9.2).

How the solve works, and what to try

Each time you bank a pose, the 20 mark pixels seen by both cameras join the correspondence set (corners are detected at display resolution, so they carry quantization noise — the σd of §8). The solve runs the document's §6 pipeline: normalized 8-point for F with rank-2 enforcement, E = K₂ᵀFK₁, decomposition into four (R, t) candidates with the chirality test picking the one that puts the box in front of both cameras, then triangulation of the known 1200 mm edges to convert the unit baseline into metres. Bank several varied poses and watch the baseline error fall on average — any single bank can push it up, because each pose contributes its own random draw of pixel-quantization noise and the estimate wanders as it converges (the status line explains this whenever it happens); toggle subpixel corners to remove quantization and the solve snaps to (numerically) exact — the box's 800 and 500 mm dimensions, which the solve never sees, are the honest test. In phase 2 the orange ghost marks where the solved rig believes camera 2 stands; the cloud and TSDF are built through the solved cameras only, so every millimetre of model error is inherited rig error plus quantization. Hold the box still to watch noise average down (§9.2); move it and the TSDF smears — the reason the document separates the static background from movers (§9.3–9.4).
